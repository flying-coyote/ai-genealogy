# AI-genealogy services — business-case cowork pack

## The thesis to build the case around (this is the differentiator — lead with it)
The consumer AI-genealogy market is flooded with tools that generate plausible-but-unsourced trees — the accuracy/fabrication problem is the market's open wound. This practice's edge is the opposite discipline, already documented across `PHILOSOPHY.md` and `methodology/`: **defensible, GPS-compliant, primary-sourced, retractable research** — tree *quality and defensibility* over tree *size*, evidence tiers with confidence capped by source tier, negative-search logging, and a hard human-judgment boundary (evidence weight, conflict resolution, pre-1700 identity, any platform contribution). Real assets to price against: three sister projects (~5,770 / 950 / 3,425 persons), 500+ research sessions, a reusable methodology + starter-kit + platform guides. The business case sells *rigor as the moat* — "a genealogy you can actually defend" — not speed or volume.

## Research tasks (name a primary source + honest tier for each finding)
1. **Marketplaces & channels** — where AI-assisted or professional genealogy research is bought/sold. Investigate and compare: Upwork and Fiverr (freelance), the Association of Professional Genealogists (APG) directory, established firms as the premium reference point (Legacy Tree Genealogists, AncestryProGenealogists, Genealogists.com, Trace.com / newer entrants), niche/DNA specialists, Reddit/Facebook genealogy communities as lead sources, and an owned channel (website + Substack/newsletter + content). For each: audience, typical price band, take-rate/fees, trust signals buyers look for, and fit with a rigor-first positioning.
2. **Competitor & pricing landscape** — what full-service professional genealogy costs today (hourly vs project vs retainer), what the AI-genealogy tools charge (subscription), and where the gap is for a "human-judgment + AI-scale, GPS-standard" offering priced between DIY tools and traditional $100–200/hr professional research.
3. **Demand signals** — who buys genealogy research and why (heritage/identity, estate/probate & heir tracing, citizenship-by-descent applications, DNA-match resolution, adoptee/unknown-parentage, lineage-society applications e.g. DAR/SAR, family-history gifts). Rank segments by willingness-to-pay × how well AI-scaled-but-sourced research serves them.
4. **The accuracy/ethics problem as positioning** — document the market's fabrication problem with real examples, then map exactly how this methodology answers it (source tiers, GPS elements, negative-search logging, contribution gates). This becomes the sales narrative and the guarantee language.

## Service design (the deliverable this case must produce)
- **Full-service tiers:** a complete direct-line genealogical run + a written, sourced family-history narrative (the flagship). Define 2–3 depth tiers (e.g., N generations, N sourced persons, GPS-readiness per person) with clear scope boundaries and turnaround.
- **Microservices (productized, fixed-price):** single brick-wall / one-candidate research; one lineage line extended; record retrieval + citation; DNA-match interpretation; a written 1-page sourced ancestor biography; and a distinctive one for the rigor brand — a **tree-defensibility audit** (take a client's existing Ancestry tree and grade it against the GPS standard, flag Tier-5 cascades and impossible dates, hand back a prioritized fix list). This last one productizes the exact integrity-checking the methodology already does.
- For each service: scope, price, what AI accelerates vs what needs human judgment, turnaround, and the honest limits (below).

## Honest constraints the plan must state (do not let the case overpromise)
- GPS compliance bounds what can be promised — some brick walls are permanent; "we'll find your ancestor" is not a deliverable, "we'll do a reasonably exhaustive, fully-sourced search and tell you what the evidence supports" is.
- The human-judgment boundary from `PHILOSOPHY.md` stays — evidence weight, conflict resolution, pre-1700/nobility identity, and any platform contribution are human calls; the offering is AI-scaled research, not autonomous research.
- Living-person privacy and platform Terms of Service (Ancestry/FamilySearch scraping limits, DNA-data handling, GDPR/CCPA for client data) are real constraints — flag them, don't hand-wave.
- Price/position on defensibility and sourcing, never on "cheaper because AI" — racing traditional research to the bottom on price undercuts the whole rigor thesis.

## Output
- A business-case / plan document in this repo — propose `business/BUSINESS-CASE-2026-07-09.md` — with: the positioning thesis, a marketplace comparison matrix, the service + pricing menu (full-service tiers + microservices), segment prioritization, unit economics / rough pricing math, a go-to-market first-90-days sketch (which channel first, first-client path, what to productize from the existing starter-kit), and the constraints/ethics section.
